A [[United States Marine Corps|Marine Corps]] 


> The Marine Corps has awarded a $19 million contract for 10 Tempest counter-drone vehicles that are deliberately built to shoot and then leave. The system fires [[AGM-114 Hellfire|AGM-114L Longbow Hellfire]] missiles at Class 2 and Class 3 drones, then withdraws while the missiles are still in flight so the crew spends as little time as possible in the kill zone.

> A Pentagon contract announcement dated August 11 confirmed the award to Vertex Modernization and Sustainment, a V2X company based in Indianapolis. The deal covers the vehicles plus software, spare parts, training, and support services. Work is scheduled to run through August 10, 2027.

> The $19,062,156 hybrid contract is structured as [[Firm-Fixed Pricing]], [[Cost-Plus-Fixed-Fee]], and cost [[Indefinite Delivery, Indefinite Quantity]]. The [[Portfolio Acquisition Executive]] Marine Corps, Program Manager Ground Based Air Defense in Quantico, Virginia, is the contracting activity. The order falls under the Denied Area Sprinter-Hellfire, or ==DASH==, effort.

> Tempest starts with a commercial Can-Am Maverick X3 chassis. The off-road buggy is fitted with dual missile launchers and a sensor suite that detects, tracks, and engages drones. The company lists several design priorities: fire-and-forget operations, dual launchers for flexible engagement profiles, a counter-unmanned aerial system suite effective against Class 2 and Class 3 drones in degraded GPS and communications environments, all-weather performance, and commercial-off-the-shelf components that are easier and cheaper to maintain. Trailer-mounted stationary versions are also available for fixed-site defense.
