In the [[United States Navy|Navy]], this is somewhat their analogue for [[Military Occupational Specialty|MOS]]... sort of. Honestly the Navy way of doing these things seems fucking annoying and complicated.


Terms for Enlisted folks:
1. Rating: Broad occupational title (BM for Boatwain's Mate, IT for Information Systems Technician)
2. Rates: Combination of the rating and paygrade (BM2 is a Boatswain's Mate Second Class, an E-5 Petty Officer)
3. Navy Enlisted Classification (NEC) Code: A sub-speciality code that identifies specific skills or training beyond the general rating.

Example:
- Rating: HM (Hospital Corpsman)
- Rate: HM3 (Hospital Corpsman Third Class, indicating a sailor at the E-4 paygrade)
- NEC Code: L03A (Field Medical Service Technician, meaning the corpsman has completed advanced training to serve combat roles.)

==In contrast==, Navy officers use the Navy Officer Occupational Classification System (NOOCS), which equivalents:
1. Designator (Equivalent of a Rating): A four-digit Designator Code, identifying their primary warfare community, administrative category, and commission status. 1110 is for a regular active-duty [[Surface Warfare Officer]] (SWO).
2. Rank (The Equivalent of a Rate): Officers use standard military ranks (Ensign, Lieutenant, Captain) to show authority and paygrade. 
3. Navy Officer Billet Classification (NOBC, equivalent of a NEC): A four digit code that reflects the occupational experience and functional duties an officer has accumulated across command tours.
4. Additional Qualification Designation (AQD): A three-character alphanumeric code reflecting certifications, unique combat qualifications, or specific training tiers attained outside their baseline designator.
