Gives the ability to deconflict air and ground assets without relying on a [[Fire Support Coordination Line|FSCL]] of battle to prevent friendly fire. Rather than a linear battle that commences and proceeds at an uncertain pace, ROZ operations utilize the element of surprise to open a column  of battlespace for a predetermined length of time, conduct operations in that battlespace, and collapse it as the mission concludes.

ROZ warfare evolved out of the US counterinsurgency operations in Afghanistan and Iraq; instead of a linear construct, the battlefield was partitioned into mission-specific horizontally and vertically defined [[Kill Box]]es.
- The advantage of a kill box over a linear battlefield construct like a [[Fire Support Coordination Line]] is that it enables a faster ops-tempo by creating a predesignated zone of [[Deconfliction]]. 


Over time, [[Joint Special Operations Command|JSOC]] moved away from employing [[Kill Box]]es in kill/capture missions, and instead began using more customizable [[Restricted Operating Zone]]s (ROZs). In contrast to the fixed latitudinal and longitudinal coordinates for a kill-box, the ability to control an air stack from the ground in real time enables ROZs to become temporally and geographically flexible columns of battlespaces. Digital networking between air and ground components made real-time coordination possible; [[Joint Terminal Attack Controller|JTAC]]s with handheld video receivers gave operators on the ground access to aerial view, letting them direct an entire stack of air assets from CAS to UAVs, fighters, and satellites.
- Linear Battlefield: Geographic two-dimensional [[Fire Support Coordination Line]] to allow procedural ground-air coordination.
- Kill Box: Geographic three-dimensional space predefined boxes  recognizing temporal and spatial discontinuities in the progress of the plan; still relies on convention, rather than networking for deconfliction.
- ROZ Warfare: Customized, four-dimensional "Aggreagate-Operate-Disaggregate" network-centered operations that are synchronized and deconflicted using visual communications and technology.
	-  Kill boxes generally facilitate routine strikes, deep interdiction, and large-scale coordination between air and ground assets. ROZs are frequently built for precise, dynamic operations, such as a localized Special Operations Forces (SOF) raid, an intelligence gathering drone flight, or an active medical evacuation route.
