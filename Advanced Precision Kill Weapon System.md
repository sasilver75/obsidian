A kit that equips [[Hydra 70]] unguided rockets with laser guidance, and turns them into precision-guided munitions (PGMs).
This original APKWS version ==never entered service with the US,== (though it did with allies, e.g. Ukraine) but an upgraded version, ==the APKWS II, did!== (The APKWS II is still Hydra 70-based)

2002-2021: Development and testing of the APKWS.

In 2025, the [[Advanced Precision Kill Weapon System|APKWS II]] variant was revealed, featuring a passive infrared sensors as well as the original's laser-guidance, allowing the weapon to be fired with "anoint and shoot" capability (the target is initially lased to orient the missile, but the lase is not maintained, and the infrared sensor takes over for terminal guidance) to increase rate of fire, particularly against large numbers of cruise missiles or UAS targets. Development is expected to be completed by the end of 2026.

> While an [[AIM-120 AMRAAM]] is $1-1.3M/apiece, and the [[AIM-9 Sidewinder]] is ~$472k... these are too expensive to be using against enemy affordable mass. The cost/exchange ratio is not good enough, and because of the complexity of air intercept, it's generally expected that interceptors should be twice as expensive as the thing they're shooting down (2:1). In reality, we're often 4-40:1 against the Houthis to defend shipping lanes. 
> The Navy expended something like 400 individual munitions taking on Houthi drones/missiles, ranging from $1.5M-12M each. This shakes out to ~$780M in expended munitions... and up until recently, the US lacked the ability to reload its interceptors while at sea, and today that capability exists but is pretty limited.
> This is where the [[Advanced Precision Kill Weapon System|AGR-20 FALCO]] comes in; rather than using multi-million dollar interceptors... the AGR-20 is effectively a cheap 70mm Hydra rocket married up to a laser guidance system ripped out of the Hellfire missile. This system takes an existing rocket and adds a soda-can-sized mid-body guidance system behind the warhead but ahead of the rocket motor.
