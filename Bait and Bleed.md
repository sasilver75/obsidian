A military strategy coined by John Mearsheimer in 2001 as a [[Strategy]] where a great power covertly provokes rival states into prolonged mutual conflict, allowing them to exhaust military, economic, and political resources while the instigator preserves its strength and later capitalizes on their rival's debilitation.

> The aim is to induce rival states to engage in a protracted [war of attrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attrition_warfare "Attrition warfare") against each other "so that they bleed each other white", while the baiter who encouraged the conflict remains on the sidelines and maintains its military strength.


> Mearsheimer describes a similar strategy which he calls "bloodletting", which does not involve incitement or baiting by a third party. When a state's rivals have already gone to war independently, the aim is to encourage the conflict to continue as long as possible to let the rival states weaken or "bleed" each other's military strength, while the bloodletting party stays out of the fighting.
