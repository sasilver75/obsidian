A classified program imposing access and safeguarding controls beyond those normally required at its classification level. 
- SAPs are *not* a classification level, and may contain [[Confidential Classification|Confidential]], [[Secret Classification|Secret]], or [[Top Secret Classification|Top Secret]] information.
- ==A security clearance alone does not grant access==; access also requires a need-to-know and specific authorization.


The most restrictive protection framework in the US government
- Applies enhanced controls beyond normal classification
- Limited access list
- Dedicated secucrity officers
- Separate facilities


The existence of the program itself may be classified.
To get in requires a formal nomination and approval by the program's own authority.



Some SAPs are "Acknowledged SAPs", which are confirmed to exist, with its name, budget line, and general purpose appearing in public documents. The [[B-2 Spirit|B-2]] operated as an Acked SAP once it was made public.
- The opposite of this is an "Unacknowledged SAP"; the [[F-117 Nighthawk]] operated as one for over a decade before being publicly revealed in 1988.
- A "Waived SAP" is the most restricted category; Congressional notification is waived entirely except for 8 individuals: The chairs and ranking members of the four defense committees:
	- [[United States Senate Committee on Armed Services|Senate Armed Services Committee]]
	- [[United States Senate Committee on Appropriations|Senate Appropriations Committee]]
	- [[United States House Committee on Armed Services|House Armed Services Committee]]
	- [[United States House Committee on Appropriations|House Appropriations Committee]]
	- There's no public accounting of how many of these programs exist or what they cost.
