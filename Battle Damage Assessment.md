The process of evaluating the physical and functional damage inflicted on a target as a result of military operations. A core component of combat assessment, and used to inform judgements about mission effectiveness and optential follow-on actions, including reattack recommendations.

Objectives:
- Did or capability deliver the desired [[Effect]]s on target?
- What was the extent of the effect at the target element, [[Targeting|Target]], and [[Target Systems Analysis|Target System]] level?
- Was there additional or [[Collateral Damage]] that should be reported?
- Were the commander's targeting objectives met?
- What are the intelligence inputs for for [[Munitions Effectiveness Assessment]] (MEA)?
- What are the intelligence inputs for [[Reattack Recommendation|RR]] or future targeting recommendations?


BDAs are defined by three phases:
	1. The initial target assessment reporting on physical damage assessment (PDA) of the target. This BDA level phase is often derived from single-source reporting (e.g. sortie debrief, [[Weapon System Video|WSV]], initial imagery report).
	2. The initial [[Functional Damage Assessment]] (FDA) of the target, and an estimate of the recuperation time required to repair or reconstitute the target's function. Often derived from the assessed physical damage and all-source intelligence.
	3. The [[Target System Damage Assessment]] (TSDA), which encompasses all-source assessments of the change and remaining target system functional capabilities and capacities relative to the targeting objectives after military operations began. Based on the FDAs of individual targets within the system, and changes driven by second and third-order effects. Normally produced by national-level intelligence working closely with joint assessment teams ([[J2 Officer|J-2]], [[J3 Officer|J-3]], [[J5 Officer|J-5]]).
