The Air Tasking Cycle is the time-bound process used by a joint air component to translate commander's guidance into planned, scheduled, executed, and assessed air operations. 

Compared to the larger [[Joint Targeting Cycle]] (which is more about targeting logic), the Air Tasking Cycle is more about airpower execution management.

How a theater air component decides what air missions should happen, assigns aircraft and supporting assets to those missions, publishes the [[Air Tasking Order]], executes it, and assesses the results. So again, the ATO is a *product of the Air Tasking Cycle.*

Think of the Air Tasking Cycle as an operational production line: Commander's intent -> air guidance -> target nomination -> weaponeering -> force allocation -> air tasking order -> mission execution -> assessment -> next cycle.

The cycle is usually associated with an [[Air Tasking Order]] period, often a 24-hour execution window. The most important part is that multiple ATOs are usually in motion at once; once one is being executed, one being finalized, one being built, one being shaped by strategy, and one being assessed.

_______

An analytical, systematic cycle focusing air efforts on accomplishing operational requirements. Much of the cycle is conducted through an interrelated series of information exchanges and active involvement in planning, target development, air execution, and assessment.

Unlike the [[Joint Targeting Cycle]], the air tasking cycle ==in constrained by time;== the six stages of the air tasking cycle promote flexibility and versatility with a series of [[Air Tasking Order]]s and related products in progress at once, and by responding during execution to changes in the operating environment.

Air Tasking Cycle Stages
### Stage 1: Objectives, Effects, and Guidance
- The air component develops a [[Joint Air Operations Plan]] (JAOP) that supports the overall JFC's objectives. 
- The implement guidance, the JFC establishes a [[Battle Rhythm]], a series of recurring meetings/working groups to integrate the targeting process across the joint force. Two such forums are the JTCB and JCMB.
	1. [[Joint Targeting Coordination Board]] (JTCB): A group formed by the JFC to perform broad targeting oversight functions, typically chaired by the deputy JFC, the [[J3 Officer|J-3]], or a component commander. The JTCB provides a forum where components synchronize their targeting priorities and activities with their respective schemes of maneuver, ensuring all efforts are integrated to meet the JFC's objectives. Representatives from the [[Air Operations Center|AOC]]'s [[Strategy Division]] (SRD) attend the JTCB to provide input on air component operations and, in turn, receive JFC guidance/intent/apportionment decisions. The SRD determines the expected effort, expressed as percentage or priority, to be devoted to the various air operations for a given period. The [[Air Operations Directive]] (AOD) development starts with the JTCB. The SRD drafts the AOD for air component commander approval. The AOD is the primary vehicle for the air component commander to express intent, communicate the JFC's air apportionment decision, and outline desired effects for targeting. The AOD provides detailed, logical lists of effects-based tasks, ISR collection requirements target prioritization, and guidance on considerations for [[Time Sensitive Target|Time-Sensitive Target]]. [[Measures of Performance]] (MOP) and [[Measures of Effectiveness]] (MOE) are typically listed for collection requirements and to appropriately assess operations.
	2. [[Joint Collection Management Board]] (JCMB): The JCMB is a group formed by the JFC to serve as a joint forum managing collection requests and coordinating collection operations. The JCMB chair is typically the [[J2 Officer|J-2]], and co-chaired by the [[J3 Officer|J-3]].

### Stage 2: Target Development
- The bulk of target development is completed by phase 2 of the continuous Joint Targeting Cycle. In the time-bound air tasking cycle, the air component receives targeting guidance along with the [[Joint Target List]] (JTL), the [[Restricted Target List]] (RTL), and the [[No-Strike List]] (NSL) from the [[Joint Targeting Coordination Board|JTCB]].
	- A JTL is a list of validated targets with no target engagement restrictions, whereas the RTL contains validated targets with engagement restrictions. The NSL isn't a target list, but contains a list of entities that are protected from the effects of military operations under international low or [[Rules of Engagement|ROE]].
- The Component's [[Target Nomination List]] (TNL): Guided by the desired effects listed in the [[Air Operations Directive|AOD]], the targeting effects team (TET) within the [[Combat Plans Division]] analyzes the menu provided by the [[Joint Target List|JTL]] and [[Restricted Target List|RTL]] and avoids entities listed on the [[No-Strike List|NSL]]. The TET coordinates with other AOC teams within the information warfare team (IWT) and the [[Intelligence, Surveillance, and Reconnaissance Division|ISRD]] to select targets that best achieve the guidance listed in the AOD. This collaborative effort results in the creation of the air component [[Target Nomination List|TNL]]. Once approved by the air component commander, the TNL is sent to the [[Joint Fires Element|JFE]] for inclusion in [[Joint Integrated Prioritized Target List|JIPTL]], a prioritized list of targets approved by the JFC leading the ATO process. After [[Joint Targeting Coordination Board|JTCB]] review, the JFC approves the JPITL.
- [[Target Development Nomination]] (TDN): During this phase, the air component can submit TDNs to the JFC for inclusion on the [[Candidate Target List]] (CTL). The submitted TDN is developed, vetted, and validated during the joint targeting cycle, then submitted to the JFC for approval. If rejected, the TDN can be subject to further development, but if approved, can be placed on the [[Joint Target List|JTL]], [[Restricted Target List|RTL]], or [[Global Integrated Target List|GITL]]. 
- [[Component Prioritized Collection List]] (CPCL): In parallel with [[Joint Targeting Coordination Board|JTCB]], the [[Joint Collection Management Board|JCMB]] propagates collection guidance to the components. Based on this guidance, the [[Air Operations Center|AOC]]'s [[Intelligence, Surveillance, and Reconnaissance Division|ISRD]] reviews the [[Air Operations Directive|AOD]] and then begins identifying collection and exploitation requirements.
	- Targeteers work with collection managers to ensure target development, prestrike, and poststrike requirements are integrated into the collection plan.
- [[Joint Integrated Prioritized Collection List]] (JIPCL): Once their air component commander has approved the [[Component Prioritized Collection List|CPCL]], the [[Intelligence, Surveillance, and Reconnaissance Division|ISRD]] forwards it to the [[Joint Collection Management Board|JCMB]] for inclusion in the [[Joint Integrated Prioritized Collection List|JIPCL]]. The JIPCL is a prioritized list of intelligence collection and exploitation requirements needed to support future development efforts and the measure whether desired effects.)

### Stage 3: Weaponeering and Allocation
- Weaponeering and allocation function together to produce the [[Master Air Attack Plan]], and are integral to targeting. During this stage, the approved [[Joint Integrated Prioritized Target List|JIPTL]] is pushed to the air component by the [[Joint Fires Element|JFE]]. Coordination for both weaponeering and allocation commences before this final [[Joint Integrated Prioritized Target List|JIPTL]] approval and continues into execution planning.
- [[Weaponeering]]: This is the process of determining the specific means required to create a desired effect on a given target. Identifies the whole range of engagement options that may affect the target and highlights or selects the most appropriate engagement capabilities commensurate with desired effects for each phase of the campaign.
	- It does not predict the outcome of every munition delivery, but represents statistical averages based on modeling, weapons tests, and real-world experience. 
	- Usually done (for air targets) by the Targets and Tactical Assessment (TTA) team from the [[Intelligence, Surveillance, and Reconnaissance Division|ISRD]].
	- The final weaponeering solution is chosen by the [[Master Air Attack Plan|MAAP]] team.
- Some weapons/capabilities require long lead times in planning, deployment, and approval, so they should be considered early and included at the beginning of the [[Joint Planning Process for Air|JPPA]] .
- Precautions should be taken toi avoid collateral damage and harm to civilians, other persons, and protected objects.
- [[Master Air Attack Plan]] (MAAP): The air component commander's time-phased air component scheme of maneuver for a given ATO period, synchronizing commander's guidance, desired effects, supported components' schemes of maneuver, friendly capabilities, and likely enemy [[Course of Action|COA]]s, and allocating friendly resources against approved targets.

### Stage 4: ATO Production and Dissemination
- Finalization of the ATO and associated orders and dissemination to combat units.
- The ATO Production Rhythm figure below shows that on any given execution day, five ATOs are either being developed or processed.
- This stage is based on the commander's guidance (especially the [[Air Operations Directive|AOD]]), the [[Master Air Attack Plan|MAAP]], and component requirements. The [[Air Tasking Order|ATO]], [[Airspace Control Order|ACO]], and [[Special Instructions|SPINS]] provide operational and tactical direction at appropriate levels of detail.

### Stage 6: Assessment
- The continuous process of measuring the overall effectiveness of joint force capabilities during military operations, and one of the four major [[Command and Control|C2]] activities.
- Helps answer basic questions:
	- "Are we doing the right things?"
	- "Are we doing things right?"
	- "Are we measuring the right things?"
- [[Battle Damage Assessment]] (BDA): The purpose of a BDA is to estimate target damage or effects resulting from the application of either kinetic or non-kinetic capabilities.
	- A coordinated and integrated effort between intelligence and operations functions.
	- BDAs are defined by three phases:
		1. The initial target assessment reporting on physical damage assessment (PDA) of the target.
		2. The initial [[Functional Damage Assessment]] (FDA) of the target, and an estimate of the recuperation time required to repair or reconstitute the target's function. 
		3. The [[Target System Damage Assessment]] (TSDA), which encompasses all-source assessments of the change and remaining target system functional capabilities and capacities relative to the targeting objectives after military operations began.
- [[Collateral Damage Assessment]] (CDA) occurs to compare [[Collateral Damage Estimation|CDE]] with observed damage.
- [[Munitions Effectiveness Assessment]] (MEA) occurs to evaluate if a munition performed as intended.
- [[Reattack Recommendation]]s are issued after merging the picture of what was done ([[Battle Damage Assessment|BDA]]) with how it was done ([[Munitions Effectiveness Assessment|MEA]]) and comparing the result with [[Measures of Effectiveness|MOE]]s.

Deliberate vs Dynamic Target Processes in the Air Tasking Cycle
- [[Deliberate Targeting|Deliberate On-Call Target]]s are included in the ATO along with the planned targets, but since the coordinates are known, no precise engagement time is listed.
- Unscheduled targets ((those that have some deliberate planning behind them, but aren't being actioned on in the current cycle)) are developed similarly to on-call targets but do not meet the priority to make the ATO and are accounted for in planning in case they are detected.
- Overall, both deliberate and dynamic targeting adhere to target development standards throughout the six stages of the air tasking cycle.
