The Air Tasking Cycle is the time-bound process used by a joint air component to translate commander's guidance into planned, scheduled, executed, and assessed air operations. 

Compared to the larger [[Joint Targeting Cycle]] (which is more about targeting logic), the Air Tasking Cycle is more about airpower execution management.

How a theater air component decides what air missions should happen, assigns aircraft and supporting assets to those missions, publishes the [[Air Tasking Order]], executes it, and assesses the results. So again, the ATO is a *product of the Air Tasking Cycle.*

Think of the Air Tasking Cycle as an operational production line: Commander's intent -> air guidance -> target nomination -> weaponeering -> force allocation -> air tasking order -> mission execution -> assessment -> next cycle.

The cycle is usually associated with an [[Air Tasking Order]] period, often a 24-hour execution window. The most important part is that multiple ATOs are usually in motion at once; once one is being executed, one being finalized, one being built, one being shaped by strategy, and one being assessed.
