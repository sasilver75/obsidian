A highly centralized, rigid, and detailed planning cycle over a defined period, commonly a 24-72 period, used to sequence and direct combat air operations.

In contrast, [[Mission-Type Orders]] Tasking is a decentralized approach where commanders dictate *what* needs to be accomplished, but give subordinate units the autonomy to decide *how* to do it.

Key idea: Airpower is fast, but the ***support system is scarce and interdependent***.
- Strike aircraft, tankers, [[Intelligence, Surveillance, and Reconnaissance|ISR]], [[Electronic Warfare]], [[Suppression of Enemy Air Defenses|SEAD]], defensive counterair, rescue coverage, airspace control, and target approvals all have to line up.
- The ATO is the synchronization mechanism that says: "Which unit flies what mission, when, with what support, against what target or area, under what constraints?"

Still, [[Dynamic Targeting]], on-call aircraft, alert aircraft, retasking, and fragmentary changes let commanders adapt during execution.
- The ATO is best understood as the baseline plan for coordinated air operations, not as the whole execution reality. Dynamic targeting and subordinate initiative happen against that baseline.
