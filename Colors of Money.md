In the context of [[Defense Procurement]], colors of money refers to informal [[Department of Defense|Department of Defense]] language for the different appropriations that Congress provides for different purposes.

The central idea:
> Congress doesn't give the DoD one interchangeable pile of money; Congress provides separate appropriations for developing systems, buying systems, operating systems, paying personnel, and constructing facilities.

Each appropriation has its own:
- Authorized purpose
- Period during which it may be obligated
- Statutory and congressional restrictions
- Budget documentation
- Rules governing transfers and reprogramming
- Funding policies

Consequently, having enough total dollars doesn't necessarily mean that a program has the correct money available.

A program with $20M of unused procurement funding generally can't decide to use that money for contractor engineering work properly chargeable to Research, Development, Test, and Evaluation; the program might have money, but it has the wrong *color* of money.

# The Major Colors
- ==Military Personnel== (MILPERS): Pay and personnel-related costs for military members, typically 1 fiscal year obligation availability.
- ==Operation and Maintenance== (O&M): Operating, sustaining, maintaining, and administering forces (1 fiscal year)
- ==Research, Development, Test, and Evaluation== (RDT&E): Developing, integrating, demonstrating, and testing new capabilities (2 fiscal years)
- ==Procurement==: Buying production or operational end items and investment equipment (~3 fiscal years)
- ==Shipbuilding and Conversion, Navy== (SCN): Constructing and converting Navy ships (~5 fiscal years)
- ==Military Construction== (MILCON): Constructing, altering, or acquiring military real property facilities (~5 fiscal years)
- ==Military Family Housing== (Family Housing): Construction and operation of military family housing (varies)
- ==Defense Working Capital Fund== (DWCF): Revolving fund activities that sell goods/services to DoD customers (generally no-year revolving funds)
