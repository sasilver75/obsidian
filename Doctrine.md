
A organization's official body of principles for how it thinks, plans, decides, and acts in a domain.

Usually authoritative guidance, rather than a rigid script. The shared concepts/terminology/roles/processes/default methods let different units coordinate and communicate without having to reinvent the wheel each time.

> "When facing this problem, this is how we generally understand it, organize around it, talk about it, and solve it."

For example, fires doctrine explains how commanders prioritize targets, request fires, coordinate airspace, avoid fratricide, assign authorities, and assess effects.
