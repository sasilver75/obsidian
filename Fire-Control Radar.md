A [[Radar]] designed to provide information to a [[Fire-Control System]] to direct weapons such that they hit a target.

It is a specialized terminal-stage subset of [[Target Acquisition Radar|Targeting Radar]] that tracks the target and continuously calculates the exact
- Targeting Radar: Responsible for detecting, acquiring, and "illuminating" an enemy target, using narrower, more-focused beams than long-range [[Detection And Search Radar|Search Radar]]s.
- Fire Control Radar: Takes over after the targeting radar has acquired the threat, locking onto the target and continuously tracking its range, speed, and exact trajectory in real time, then integrating with a FCS to calculate exact firing angles, and providing continuous mid-course or terminal guidance commands that a missile needs to hit a highly maneuverable target.

Fire Control Radars operate in three different phases:
1. ==Designation or Vectoring Phase==: The fire-control radar must be directed to the general location of the target due to the radar's narrow beam width. This phase is also called "lighting up." It ends when lock-on is acquired.
2. ==Acquisition Phase==: The FCR searches in the designated area in a predetermined search pattern until the target is located or redesignated. This phase terminates when a weapon is launched.
3. ==Tracking Phase==: The fire-control radar enters into the track phase is located; this phase ends when the target is destroyed.


Note: Some modern radars have a ==track-while-scan== capability, enabling them to function simultaneously as both a [[Fire-Control Radar]] and a [[Detection And Search Radar|Search Radar]]. This works either by having the radar switch between sweeping the search sector and sending directed pulses at the target to be tracked, or by using a [[Phased Array]] antenna to generate multiple simultaneous radar beams that both search and track.
