The process of converting a strategic need into a usable, maintainable, affordable military capability over decades. Most failures happen because governments optimize one part of that chain while damaging another.

A simplified lifecycle:
- Strategic Need
- Capability Requirement
- Technical Specification
- Competition and Contracting
- Development and Integration
- Production and Fielding
- Sustainment

It's important to understand that ==the weapon system itself is only one input into the capability that you're trying to gain/bolster==. You need more than anti-submarine helicopters, you need pilots, bases, maintenance systems, logistics, command structures, spare parts, training pipelines, weapons, software support, and doctrine.

How to do procurement badly:
- Start with the wrong requirement
- Gold-Plate the system
- Turn mature products back into development programs
- Assign risk to the wrong party
- Ignore sustainment and intellectual property
- Allow scope creep after contract award
- Move too fast or too slowly
- Cut quantities to save money

How to do procurement well:
- Be ruthless about the real military problem
- Prefer mature, existing solutions when they are good enough
- Design for quantity, maintainability, and training
- Treat sustainment as part of the original purchase
- Put risk where it can be managed
- Stabilize demand
- Know when to cancel, scale, or export


# Terms
- Capability: The ability to achieve an operational effect, not merely posession of equipment.
- Capability Gap: The difference between what hte force can currently do and what the force needs to be able to do.
- Capability Need: The operational need that justifies pursuing a new or improved capability.
- [[Fundamental Inputs to Capability]]: The full set of inputs needed to make a capability real: personnel, training, facilities, logistics, doctrine, organization, command systems, sustainment, and the major system itself.
- Requirement: A statement of what the military needs the system or capability to do.
- Operational Requirement: A requirement stated in military-effect terms, such as range, survivability, payload, readiness, or response time.
- Technical Requirement: A requirement stated in engineering terms, such as weight, power draw, interface standards, encryption standards, or temperature tolerance.
- Threshold Requirement: The minimum acceptable value.
- Objective Requirement: The desired higher value if achievable within cost, schedule, and risk.
- Specification: A more technical description of required characteristics, interfaces, standards, performance, or design constraints.
- Key Performance Parameter: A critical performance requirement that the system *must* meet.
- Key System Attribute: An important attribute, but usually less decisive than a key performance parameter.
- [[Program of Record]]: An officially recognized, budgeted, planned acquisition program.
- Acquisition Program: The managed effort to develop, buy, field, and sustain a system.
- [[Initial Operating Capability]]: The point at which the system is usable by an operational unit at a minimum useful level.
- [[Full Operating Capability]]: Planned full capability is available across the intended force.
- Full Operating Capability: The point at which the planned capability is fully fielded, trained, supported, and operational.
- In-Service Date: The date a system formally enters service.
- [[Sustainment]]: The maintenance, repair, supply, software support, upgrades, training support, and logistics needed to keep the systems usable.
- Trade Space: The set of things designers can trade against each other: cost, weight, range, speed, armor, payload, schedule, maintainability, and so on.
- Gold-Plating: Adding excessive or "nice to have" requirements until the system becomes too expensive, late, or risky.
- Scope Creep: Growth in required features after the program has started.
- Appropriation: Money legally authorized for a particular category of spending.
- [[Technology Readiness Level]]: A scale for how mature a technology is, from basic research to operationally proven.
- Manufacturing Readiness Level: A scale for how ready the production process is.
- Demonstrator: A system built to prove a technology or concept can work.
- Preliminary Design Review: An earlier review checking whether a basic design approach is sound.
- Critical Design Review: A major review checking whether the design is mature enough to proceed.
- Design Freeze: A point where design changes are restricted to prevent endless churn.
- Integration Risk: The risk that individually working subsystems fail when combined into the whole system.
- Request for Information: A market research request asking industry what is possible.
- Request for Proposal: A formal request asking bidders to submit offers.
- Request for Tender: Similar to Request for Proposal; more common in some Commonwealth systems.
- Source Selection: The process of choosing the winning bidder.
- Prime Contractor: The main contractor responsible for delivering the system.
- Subcontractor: A company supplying parts, services, or subsystems to the prime contractor.
- [[Firm-Fixed Pricing]]: Contractor agrees to deliver at a set price, with more contractor risk.
- [[Cost-Plus Pricing]]: Government pays allowable costs plus a fee, with more government risk.
- Incentive Contract: Uses bonuses or penalties to shape cost, schedule, or performance behavior.
- Low-Rate Initial Production: Early limited production before full-rate production approval.
- Full-Rate Production: Large-scale production after testing and approval.
- Industrial Base: The network of companies, workers, facilities, suppliers, and skills needed to produce defense equipment.
- Minimum Sustaining Rate: The minimum production rate needed to keep a production line, workforce, or supplier base alive.
- Block Upgrade: A planned package of improvements added in batches over time.
- Developmental Test and Evaluation: Testing to determine whether a system meets technical requirements.
- Operational Test and Evaluation: Testing to determine whether real users can use the system effectively in realistic conditions.
- Initial Operational Test and Evaluation: A major operational test before full production decisions.
- Acceptance Testing: Testing used to decide whether the government accepts delivered systems.
- Milestone A: Decision to enter technology maturation and risk reduction.
- Milestone B: Decision to enter engineering and manufacturing development.
- Milestone C: Decision to enter production and deployment.
- [[Mission Capable Rate]]: Percentage of time systems can perform at least one assigned mission.
- [[Fully Mission Capable Rate]]: Percentage of time systems can perform all assigned missions.
- Operational Availability: Probability that the system is available when needed in real operating conditions.
