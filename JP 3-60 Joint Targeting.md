# Chapter 1: Overview

## Introduction
- [[Targeting]] is the process of selecting and prioritizing targets and matching the appropriate response to them, considering operational requirements and capabilities.
	- It requires a continuous, analytic process to identify/develop/affect targets to meet commander objectives.
- A [[Targeting|Target]] is an entity or object that performs a function for the adversary, considered for possible engagement or action. Its importance derives from its potential contribution to achieving a friendly commander's objectives.
	- Described by Physical, Functional, Cognitive and control, Environmental, and Temporal categories/characteristics.
- The [[Joint Targeting Cycle]] provides an iterative, logical methodology for the development, planning, execution, and assessment of targeting, weapons, and capabilities effectiveness.
- Principles of targeting:
	- ==Focused==: Efficiently achieve the [[Joint Force Commander]] (JFC)'s objectives through target engagement subject to the parameters set in the [[Concept of Operations|CONOP]], the operational limitations within the plans and orders, [[Rules of Engagement]], law of war, and agreements concerning the sovereignty of national territories.
	- ==Effects-based==: To contribute to the achievement of the JFC's objectives, targeting is concerned with the creation of specific effects through target engagement.
	- ==Interdisciplinary==: Joint targeting requires the participation of all elements of the JFC's staff, component commander's staff, other agencies, and multinational partners.
	- ==Systematic==: The joint targeting cycle is a rational and iterative process that methodically analyzes, prioritizes, and assigns assets against targets.
- [[Joint Targeting Cycle]]
	- Dependent in part on the campaign plan, contingency plan, [[Operational Order]]s, and [[Fragmentary Order]]s, which provide the context for targeting.
	- Detailed intelligence products (dynamic threat assessments, joint [[Intelligence Preparation of the Operational Environment|IPOE]]s, country assessments) facilitate detailed targeting, starting with [[Target Systems Analysis]]. Many of these intelligence products are developed/maintained/continuously updated.
	- A [[Unified Combatant Command|CCMD]] can usually provide a subordinate JFC with a list of targets applicable to a plan for a joint operations area within their [[Area of Responsibility|AOR]]
	- Targeting is usually grouped into two categories:
		- [[Deliberate Targeting]]: "Planned targets"; Produces planned targets (both scheduled and on-call targets), which are those known to exist in the operational environment with engagement actions scheduled against them. With the exception of unanticipated targets, all targets should flow from deliberate targeting.
		- [[Dynamic Targeting]]: "Targets of opportunity"; normally employed in [[Current Operations|CUOPS]] planning because the nature and timeframe associated with the current 24-hour execution period typically requires more immediate responsiveness than is achieved in deliberate targeting.
			- Prosecutes [[Time Sensitive Target|Targets of Opportunity]] that include *unscheduled targets* and *unanticipated targets*.
	- The Joint Targeting Cycle is a six-phase iterative process:
		1. ==Commander's Objectives, Targeting Guidance, and Intent==: The JFC develops and issues targeting guidance (priorities, time-sensitive targets criteria, component critical targets, target acquisition and identification criteria, authorized actions)
		2. ==Target Development and Prioritization==: Target development is the systematic examination of potential target systems, individual targets, and even elements of targets to determine the necessary type/duration of the action that must be exerted on each target to create an effect consistent with the commander's specific objectives.
		3. ==Capabilities Analysis==: This phase of the joint targeting cycle involves evaluating al available capabilities against targets' critical target elements to determine the most appropriate options available to the component commander.
		4. ==Commander's Decision and Force Assignment==: Fuses the previous capabilities analysis with available forces, sensors, and weapons systems.
		5. ==Mission Planning and Force Execution==: Detailed unit-level planning must be performed for the execution of operations. Provides component planners with direct access to detailed information on targets, and analytical reasoning that linked the target with the desired effects.
		6. ==Combat Assessment==: Assesses the effectiveness of the activities that occurred during the first five phases of the JTC.
- An [[Effect]] is the change in the physical or behavioral state of a target system, a target system component, a target, or a target element that results from an action, set of actions, or another effect.
- The [[Joint Force Commander|JFC]]'s primary targeting responsibility is in integrating/synchronizing/establishing the objectives that component commanders will achieve throughout the operational environment.
	- [[Aviation Apportionment|Apportionment]] (weight of effort) is normally proposed by the [[Joint Force Air Component Commander]] (JFACC), and approved by the [[Joint Force Commander|JFC]].
	- Joint force and component commanders identify [[High Value Target]]s (HVTs) and [[High Payoff Target]]s (HPTs) for acquisition, collection, and attack or influence.
	- The JFC promulgates intent, objective, guidance, sequencing, and priorities.
	- Maintain currency of mission planning guidance, intent, and priority commander's critical intelligence requirements throughout the operation.
	- Direct the formation, composition, and specific responsibilities of a [[Joint Fires Element]] (JFE) and [[Joint Targeting Coordination Board]] (JTCB) (if required).
