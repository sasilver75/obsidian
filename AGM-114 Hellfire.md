In Service 1984-Present

An American missile initially developed for anti-armor use, but later developed for precision drone strikes against other target types.
- Today, the primary 100-pound class air-to-ground precision weapon for the US armed forces, fielded on both surface platforms (in a surface-to-surface *and* surface-to-air role) as well as on air vehicles like [[MQ-1B Predator]], [[MQ-9 Reaper]], and rotary wing assets.

Most variants are laser-guided, though the AGM-114L "Longbow Hellfire" variant is radar-guided.
- Laser guidance can be provided either from the launcher (e.g. from an [[AH-64E Apache]] attack helicopter, other airborn target designators, or from ground-based observers.)


Variants:
- AGM-114A: Produced 1982-1992, for armored vehicles.
- AGM-114B/C: Produced 1982-1992, for armored vehicles, ship-borne targets.
- AGM-114F: Produced 1991-1994, for armored vehicles.
- AGM-114K/K2/K2A Hellfire II: Produced 1993-2018, for all armored targets.
- ==AGM-114L Longbow Hellfire==: Produced 1995-2005, 2016-Present, for all armored targets.
- AGM-114 Hellfire II (Blast Frag): Produced 1998–2010, for bunkers, light vehicles, urban soft targets.
- AGM-114N Hellfire (MAC): Produced 2003-2018, for buildings, soft-skinned targets, ship-borne targets.
- AGM-114P/P+ Hellfire II (for UAS): Produced 2003-2012, for all targets.
- ==AGM-114 Hellfire II== (Hellfire Romeo): Produced 2012-Present, for all targets.
- ==AGM-114 R9X== ("Flying Ginsu"): Variant with the kinetic warhead replaced with pop-out blades instead of explosives, used against human targets.
