Defined in Air Force Doctrine Note 1-21 (2022)

The [[United States Air Force|USAF]]'s current umbrella term for its concepts for distributed and disaggregated operations.

 New weapon systems (advances in [[Intelligence, Surveillance, and Reconnaissance|ISR]] and all-domain long-range fires) now place bases at risk that were previously considered sanctuaries. 
 
 ACE is a proactive and reactive operational ==scheme of maneuver== executed within threat timelines to ==increase survivability== while generating combat power. 
 -  ACE aims to complicate the enemy's targeting process, create political and operational dilemmas for the enemy, and create flexibility for friendly forces.
 - An operational concept that supports [[Joint All-Domain Operations]] (JADO).
 - [[Anti-Access Area Denial]] threats, reduced freedom of maneuver, and rapid proliferation of advanced technologies challenge the Air Force's ability to operate. The successful employment of ACE positions the force to OODA in concert across all domains... and present adversaries with dilemmas at an operational tempo that complicates or negates adversary responses, and enables the joint force to operate inside the adversary's decision-making cycle.

> "The best place to kill an enemy's air force is on the ground."
> - Mark Gunzinger, Director of Wargaming @ [[Mitchell Institute for Aerospace Studies]]

Key Terms:
- ==Agile==: Able to outpace adversary action through movement and maneuver to achieve commander's intent.
- ==Agile Combat Employment==: A proactive and reactive operational scheme of maneuver executed within threat timelines to increase resiliency and survivability while generating combat power.
- ==Base Cluster==: A collection of bases, geographically grouped for mutual protection and ease of C2. In the context of ACE, this typically involves the organization of an *enduring location* with one or more [[Contingency Location]]s (CLs) to establish a regional boundary, wherein the enduring location commander commands one or more CLs with appropriate uathorities to direct their activities.
- ==Conditions-Based Authorities==: A published set of authorities that are delegated down the chain of command from one commander to another, to be activated only when the specified conditions are met.
- ==Hub and Spoke Distribution==: A physical distribution system in which a major port serves as a central point from which cargo is moved to/from several radiating points to increase efficiencies.
- ==[[Mission Command]]==: An approach to [[Command and Control]] (C2) that empowers subordinate decision makers for flexibility, initiative, and responsiveness in the accomplishment of commander's intent. The core principles are: Build teams through mutual trust, create shared understanding, provide clear commander's intent, use [[Mission-Type Orders]] (MTO) when appropriate, exercise disciplined initiative, and accept prudent risk. Airmen execute mission command through *centralized command, distributed control, and decentralized execution.*
- ==Multi-Capable Airmen (MCA)==: Airmen trained in expeditionary skills and capable of accomplishing tasks outside of their core Air Force specialty ([[Air Force Speciality Code|AFSC]]); specifically, these personnel are trained as  cross-functional team to provide support to ACE force elements in an expeditionary environment.
- ==Proactive Maneuver==: A scheme of maneuver by which forces and assets are moved between operating locations to gain advantage.
- ==Reactive Maneuver==: A scheme of maneuver employed in response to observed, perceived, anticipated, or realized enemy aggression using mobility and dispersion of forces and assets to complicate enemy targeting.
- ==Threat Timelines==: Theater-specific planning factors based on the time required for an adversary to accomplish its find, fix, track, target, engage, and assess cycle.


ACE achieves its goals through the following Enablers:
1. Expeditionary and Multi-Capable Airmen
2. Mission Command
3. Tailorable force packages


# Expeditionary and Multi-Capable Airmen
- Airmen need skills that enable them to operate in contested, degraded, and operationally limited ([[Contested, Degraded, and Operationally Limited|CDO]]) environments with ==minimal support==. 
- ACE teams consist of unit-assigned multi-capable Airmen... with the ==goal of minimizing equipment and personnel footprints== to increase dispersal capabilities and complicate adversary targeting. The use of MCAs can reduce the number of people put in harm's way to generate airpower relative to traditional manning models.
# Mission Command
- In future peer conflicts, the US should not expect to achieve the air supremacy it enjoyed in recent low-intensity operations; ==every domain will be contested== and characterized by ==fluctuating levels of superiority==.
- By ==empowering subordinates at the lowest capable level to make decisions== and take decisive action at their level, mission command provides the flexibility and agility required to seize opportunities despite enemy denial or degradation of communications.
- USAF leaders must expand their operational perspective beyond their role in the [[Air Tasking Order]]. Commanders must clearly communicate their intent, and airmen must develop detailed understandings of the area of operations and how senior commanders envision winning the fight.
- Requisite details:
	- Enemy situation
	- Friendly situation
	- Joint force and air component operational priorities
	- Delegated authorities
	- Overall risk management
- These details are contained in [[Mission-Type Orders]]s, starting with a 5-paragraph [[Operational Order|OPORD]] to provide a snapshot of the commander's intent. This OPORD communicates the purpose of the operation, desired end states, the method designed to conduct it, and the resources available for execution.
- With this information subordinate leaders can make effective decisions consistent with the commander's intent, even if they've lost contact with higher echelons.
# Tailorable Force Packages
- To meet theater requirements, ACE requires tailorable force packages with the ability to execute across a range of operating locations.

> "To generate combat power from a number of locations to create dilemmas for an adversary... I just need a runway, a ramp, a weapons trailer, a fuel bladder, and a pallet of MREs. That's maybe a little bit bold, but the point is that we have to be light, lean, and agile."
> - General CQ Brown, Jr Chief of Staff of the Air Force, 2019


# ACE Framework
- ACE consists of ==five core elements==:
	1. Posture
	2. C2
	3. Movement and Maneuver
	4. Protection
	5. Sustainment

The latter four align with the [[Warfighting Function]]


### Posture
- The starting position from which all subsequent actions take place.
- Forces must be able to rapidly execute operations form various locations with integrated capabilities and interoperability across the core functions. 
- A good posture provides commanders with expanded force employment options and mitigates operational risk, enhancing defensive posture by increasing the scope and scale of friendly force locations, boosting deterrence to adversary aggression, and assuring allies by presenting a credible combat force.
- Operational unpredictability is enabled through agility of forces across pre-postured locations, increasing the number of locations that an adversary must target.
- Operational locations should be identified based on the ability to support warfighting requirements and sustainment opportunities while balancing risk to force.
- Distributed operations will exist on a spectrum, from well-developed enduring locations to potentially austere CLs.
- Required capability development includes:
	- Equipment and supply pre-positioning
	- Scalable logistics packages
	- Access to forward operating sites, including military and civil airfields.
	- Resilient communications to function in contested, degraded, or operationally limited ([[Contested, Degraded, and Operationally Limited|CDO]]) environments.
	- A force optimized for large scale combat operations in a contested environment.

### Command and Control (C2)
- Commanders in any conflict require the ability to conduct C2 across domains.

Let's step through the phases of ACE, a scheme of maneuver.
1. Setting the theater (Posture). We do this ahead of need, during competition phase, which involves prepositioning war reserve material. Forward operating sites are identified with host nations.
2. Proactive ACE maneuver. Some kind of political or security trigger causes us to disperse forces proactively from our enduring locations to our dispersed contingency locations (forward operating sites). This is to deter our adversary and assure allies.
3. Reactive ACE maneuver. To survive and regenerate, we disperse from enduring locations that are under attack. The goal is to complicate the adversary's targeting solution.
4. Mass forces from dispersed locations to take the fight to our adversary. This is intended to be effective, but not necessarily efficient, and might require innovative logistics.
5. Recovery and sustainment,. Fixing airfields rapidly, maintaining and fixing our aircraft, whether they're "Code 1" (meaning) or battle-damaged.
