A research and development agency of the US [[Department of Defense|DoD]] responsible for the development of emerging technologies for use by the military.
Originally known as the Advanced Research PRojects Agency (ARPA).


# Current Program Offices
- ==Defense Sciences Office== (DSO): Identifies and pursues high-payoff research initiatives across a broad spectrum of science and engineering disciplines (novel materials and structures, sensing and measurement, computation and processing, enabling operations, collective intelligence, global change).
- ~~Information Innovation Office~~ (I2O): Aims to ensure US technological superiority in all areas where information can provide a decisive military advantage.
	- ==Renamed== to ==Information Processing Techniques Office (IPTO)== in May 2026.
- ~~Microsystems Technology Office~~ (MTO): Development of high-performance, intelligent microsystems and next-generation components to ensure US dominance in [[Command, Control, Communications, Computers, Intelligence, Surveillance, and Reconnaissance|C4ISR]], [[Electronic Warfare|EW]], [[Directed Energy]], which depend on microsystems and components.
	- ==Renamed== to  ==Multi-X Office== in May 2026: Dominate the foundation of materials and devices, command the fight for superior sensing and computing, impose industrial sovereignty to secure our domestic advantage. ("Built on decades of breakthroughs from the Microsystems Technology Office")
- ==Strategic Technology Office== (STO, Pron. "Stow"): Focusing on technologies that have a global theater-wide impact and that involve multiple Services.
- ==Tactical Technology Office== (TTO): Engages in high-risk, high-payoff military research, emphasizing the "system" and "subsystem" approach to the development of aeronautic, space, and land systems, as well as embedded processors and control systems.
- ==Biological Technologies Office== (BTO): Fosters, demonstrates, and transitions breakthrough fundamental research, discoveries, applications integrating biology, engineering, and computer science for national security.
