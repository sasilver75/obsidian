https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_box

In joint warfare, a kill box is a three-dimensional cube of space on a battlefield where military forces and their allies are cleared to open fire on targets without the usual delays for strict, mission-by-mission coordination.

Often defined by a grid reference system based on lines of latitude and longitude, superimposed on a map of an area of operation. Each square of the grid may be sub-divided into smaller boxes, each of which may carry its own level of permission or restriction on the use of air-to-surface or surface-to-surface weapons.

Over time, [[Joint Special Operations Command|JSOC]] moved away from employing Kill Boxes in kill/capture missions, and instead began using more customizable [[Restricted Operating Zone]]s (ROZs). In contrast to the fixed latitudinal and longitudinal coordinates for a kill-box, the ability to control an air stack from the ground in real time enables ROZs to become temporally and geographically flexible columns of battlespaces. Digital networking between air and ground components made real-time coordination possible; [[Joint Terminal Attack Controller|JTAC]]s with handheld video receivers gave operators on the ground access to aerial view, letting them direct an entire stack of air assets from CAS to UAVs, fighters, and satellites.
- Linear Battlefield: Geographic two-dimensional [[Fire Support Coordination Line]] to allow procedural ground-air coordination.
- Kill Box: Geographic three-dimensional space predefined boxes  recognizing temporal and spatial discontinuities in the progress of the plan; still relies on convention, rather than networking for deconfliction.
- ROZ Warfare: Customized, four-dimensional "Aggreagate-Operate-Disaggregate" network-centered operations that are synchronized and deconflicted using visual communications and technology.
	-  Kill boxes generally facilitate routine strikes, deep interdiction, and large-scale coordination between air and ground assets. ROZs are frequently built for precise, dynamic operations, such as a localized Special Operations Forces (SOF) raid, an intelligence gathering drone flight, or an active medical evacuation route.

Kill Box Colors:
- ==Blue== (BKB): Permits air-to-surface fires in the kill-box without further coordination or deconfliction with friendly forces (assuming an aircraft is assigned to the BKB). Aircraft not assigned to the BKB are restricted from entering unless coordinated.
- ==Purple== (PKB): Reduces the coordination requirements for air-to-surface fires while still actively allowing surface component commanders to employ surface-to-surface indirect fires simultaneously. Permits air interdiction with air-to-surface fires, integrating surface-to-surface indirect and direct fires without further coordination in the Kill Box. Allows for joint fires in the Kill Box to create synergistic effects. Aircraft not assigned to the PKP are restricted from entering unless coordinated.
	- Purple allows for the integration of surface-to-surface fires *from outside the killbox,* with deconfliction using a kill box floor.

Kill Boxes are listed according to the following details:
- Type (the Kill Box Color above)
- Measure number: Value assigned by commander, 001-999
- Establishing HQ
- Affected altitudes
- Date-time group (DTG): Defines when the kill box is "established" and "cancelled"

Kill Boxes have a ==Kill Box Ceiling==, and, if Purple, have a ==Kill Box Floor== for deconfliction with any incoming surface-to-surface fires.

A kill box can be further subdivided into into what is known as a "keypad" configuration, allowing some quadrants to remain "hot" (active), while others are designated "cold." Likewise, the airspace can be designated "closed" (restricted) or "open."
