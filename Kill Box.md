In joint warfare, a kill box is a three-dimensional cube of space on a battlefield where military forces and their allies are cleared to open fire on targets without the usual delays for strict, mission-by-mission coordination.

Often defined by a grid reference system based on lines of latitude and longitude, superimposed on a map of an area of operation. Each square of the grid may be sub-divided into smaller boxes, each of which may carry its own level of permission or restriction on the use of air-to-surface or surface-to-surface weapons.
