A system used primarily by cruise missiles; uses a contour map of the terrain that is compared with measurements made during flight by an on-board [[Radar Altimeter]]. A TERCOM system considerably increases the accuracy of a missile compared with [[Inertial Navigation System]]s, and it can't be "jammed" in the way that [[Global Positioning System|GPS]] (or other [[Global Navigation Satellite System|GNSS]]s) can be.

The increased locational (and altitudinal) accuracy allows a TERCOM-equipped missile or drone to fly closer to obstacles and at generally lower altitudes, making it harder to detect by ground radar (e.g. see [[Nap-of-the-Earth]]).

The [[BGM-109 Tomahawk Land Attack Missile|Tomahawk]] in the early 1980s was using a terrain database to find their targets 1,000 miles away before [[Global Positioning System|GPS]] even existed!
Of course, now we have different models, etc. See [[Maxar|Vantor]]'s Raptor product, for instance. ((Not sure if this is more VPS than TERCOM))

# Comparison with [[Visual Positioning System]] (VPS)
- Both are GPS-independent navigation technologies used to determine location, but TERCOM matches 3D elevation maps with altitude data, while VPS matches 2D visual images with camera feeds.
