https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile

Missiles powered initially by a rocket or series of rockets in stages, but then follow an unpowered trajectory that arches upwards before descending to reach its intended target.

Ballistic Missiles have three stages of flight:
- ==Boost Phase==: Starts at launch and lasts until the rocket engine stops firing and the missile begins unpowered flight. Can last 3-5 minutes; most of this takes place in the atmosphere.
- ==Midcourse Phase==: Begins after the rocket starts firing. The missile continues to ascend towards the highest point in its trajectory, then begins the descend towards Earth. This is the longest phase of the missile's flight. For [[Intercontinental Ballistic Missile|ICBM]]s, this can last ~20 minutes. During mid-course, ICBMs can travel 24,000km (15,000 miles) an hour.
- ==Terminal Phase==: Begins when the detached warheads reenter the Earth's atmosphere and ends upon impact or detonation. During this phase, which can last for less than a minute, strategic warheads can be traveling at speeds greater than 3,200 kilometers per hour (1,988 miles per hour).

Common Ballistic Missile types, by increasing range:
- Close-Range Ballistic Missile (CRBM)
- [[Short-Range Ballistic Missile]] (SRBM)
- [[Medium-Range Ballistic Missile]] (MRBM)
- [[Intermediate-Range Ballistic Missile]] (IRBM)
- [[Intercontinental Ballistic Missile]] (ICBM)

All ballistic missiles are made up of three essential elements:
- Propulsion system: Provides the energy necessary to reach the target
	- Can either use liquid or solid propulsion systems. In general, liquid systems are somewhat more energetic than solid systems, becaues liquid fuels and oxidizers that yield more energy can be chosen. The advantage of solid propulsion systems is that they are rugged, easily stored, transportable, and have no moving parts. The trend in modern missile systems is towards the use of solid propellants because of their simplicity of operations and reduced logistical requirements.
- Guidance system: Contains steering of the missile during powered flight, and ensures the correct initial conditions for the ballistic trajectory.
	- Throughout the powered phase of flight, the instruments in the [[Inertial Navigation System]] must continually sense all of the components of the missile's acceleration; the guidance computer uses these to determine the missile's state (velocity, location, orientation) and sends corrective messages to the missile's steering system to eliminate deviations from the required flight profile.
- Payload: Destroys the target.
	- Can be nuclear, conventional, or chemical/biological (though the effectiveness of these chem-bio designs is speculative)

The accuracy of a missile is quantified by [[Circular Error Probable]] (CEP), which is defined as the radius of a circle that half of a large number of identical missiles operating without malfunction would land within.

When not surface-launched, often called [[Submarine-Launched Ballistic Missile|SLBM]]s or [[Air-Launched Ballistic Missile|ALBM]]s.
