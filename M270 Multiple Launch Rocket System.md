The [[United States Army|Army]] variant of the M270 is bassed on the chassis of the [[M3 Bradley|Bradley Cavalry Fighting Vehicle]].

> In the early 1970s, the Soviets had a clear advantage over US and NATO forces in terms of rocket artillery. Israel's use of rocket artillery in the 1973 Yom Kippur War reinforced that the US needed to be able to engage enemy air defenses and provide counter-battery fire, freeing large-caliber artillery units ot provide call-for-fire artillery support for ground forces.

Typically fires [[M270 Multiple Launch Rocket System|MLRS]] Family of Munitions (MFOM) rockets (M26, M28), [[Guided Multiple Launch Rocket System|GMLRS]] rockets (which have [[GPS INS]] and extended range), [[MGM-140 Army Tactical Missile System|ATACMS]] rockets with significant range, or even longer range [[Precision Strike Missile|PrSM]] missiles.


Variants:
- M270: Original version, weapon load of 12 rockets in two 6-pack pods.
- M270A1: Result of 2005 upgrade program; improved fire control system eand launcher mechanical system, allowing for faster launch procedures.
- M270A2: A 2019 upgrade program ot the US Army variant, including the new fire control system to allow the use of teh [[[Precision Strike Missile]] (PrSM), as well as a 600hp engine, rebuilt transmission, etc.]
