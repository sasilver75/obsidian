https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-control_system

An integrated system of components that assists a gunner in ==executing tasks needed to achieve accurate firing== by means of full or semi automation. It performs the same task as a human gunner firing a weapon, but attempts to do so faster and more accurately.

In a naval context, corrections must be made for surface wind velocity, firing ship roll and pitch, powder magazine temperature, drift of rifled projectiles, individual gun bore diameter adjusted for short-to-shot enlargement, and rate of change of range with additional modification to the firing solution based on the observation of previous shots.
- The resulting directions are known as a [[Firing Solution]], and would then be fed back out to the turrets for laying.

Modern fire-control computers allow basically any input to be added, from air density and wind, to wear on the barrels and distortion due to heating. FCSs have started appearing on smaller and smaller platforms, and are useful for aiming not just cannons, but also for aiming machine guns, small cannons, guided missiles, rifles, grenades, and rockets. They're installed on ships, submarines, aircraft, tanks, and even some small arms.
- Fire control systems are often interfaced with additional sensors ([[Sonar]], [[Radar]], [[Laser Rangefinder]]s, [[Anemometer]]s, [[Wind Vane]]s, [[Thermometer]]s, [[Barometer]]s, etc.) in order to cut down or eliminate the amount of information that must be manually entered to calculate an effective solution.
	- [[Fire-Control Radar]] is a radar designed to provide information to a FCS to direct weapons such that they hit a target.
- Once the firing solution is calculated, modern fire control systems are often also able to aim and fire the weapon, in the interest of speed and accuracy, and to let the crew (e.g. in an aircraft) perform other actions simultaneously.
