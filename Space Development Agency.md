A [[United States Space Force|Space Force]] unit tasked with deploying disruptive space technology.

Formed March 2019 to help modernized and accelerate space acquisition, whose lengthy procurement cycles plagued adoption of emerging space capabilities.
- Established by Mike Griffin, undersecretary of defense (R&E) under Trump. Griffin was a long-time advocate for LEO constellations working to eliminate US vulnerability to ballistic missiles with his work on space-based interceptors for the [[Strategic Defense Initiative]] (SDI) and [[Brilliant Pebbles]] in the 1980s.
- Wants to build the [[Combined Joint All-Domain Command and Control|JADC2]] satellite backbone.


By February 2024, the SDA had 38 satellites in orbit, and intends to have at least 1,000 satellites in [[Low Earth Orbit|LEO]] by 2026.


In May 2026, SpaceX (after previous two tranches of satellites went up) was selected for a "Space Data Network Backbone" which will accelerate the delivery of resilient, high-speed communication networks in space, $2.9B fixed-price contract.

> "A national security space architecture that provides the persistent, resilient, global, low-latency surveillance needed to deter or defeat adversary action is a prerequisite to maintaining our long-term competitive advantage."
> - Patrick Shanahan, 2019 SECDEF.'

The [[Proliferated Warfighter Space Architecture|National Defense Space Architecture]] (NDSA) grew out of these goals; a tactical [[Low Earth Orbit|LEO]] network designed to communicate missile warmings ([[Position, Navigation, and Timing|PNT]] data) and other vital information wherever it's needed on the ground as quickly and securely as possible.
- The NDSA was renamed to the [[Proliferated Warfighter Space Architecture]] (PWSA)

The NDSA/PWSA advances a network of global orbiters organized into layers, each supporting distinct military functions, such as communications, surveillance, navigation, battle management, deterrence, and missile defense, interconnected by free-space optical laser terminals in a secure [[Command and Control|C2]] optical mesh network.

> Critics have long reiterated that ground-based lasers can easily "paint" satellites in LEO, temporarily blinding their sensors, and that [[Radio Frequency|RF]] [[Jamming]] is simpler when radar satellites are in lower altitudes, since less power is needed to saturate their low-noise amplifiers. It's also easier to launch anti-satellite weapons to destroy satellites in LEO.
