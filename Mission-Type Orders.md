An order that tells a subordinate unit what effect to achieve and why, but leaves the subordinate unit meaningful freedom to decide *how to achieve it.*
> "Here is the mission, purpose, end state, constraints, and available support Use your best judgement to accomplish it."
>  An MTO gives the subordinate commander a clear purpose and desired effect, but trusts them to choose the appropriate method.

Mission-Type Orders solve the problem that {higher headquarters often cannot know enough, fast enough, to micromanage execution.} In combat, the local commander may have better information about terrain, enemy disposition, weather, timing, unit readiness, unexpected resistance, nearby friendly forces, etc. Detailed orders can become obsolete quickly; an MTO gives the subordinate commander enough intent to keep acting even when the original plan no longer fits reality.
- A good MTO still includes the assigned mission, its purpose and the commander's intent, the desired end state, relevant constraints and restraint, rules of engagements, available support, reporting requirements, and boundaries of authority.


> "If you look at a lot of past wars we've fought against large states, many of the taskings were not [[Air Tasking Order|ATO Tasking]]s, they were [[Mission-Type Orders|MTO Tasking]]s... where you'd get an MTO-type task like 'Go to this area and attrit X type of thing by 25% over the next 24 hours' instead of 'drop X weapon on Y point'. With MTO Tasking, I now have to do a minimal [[Target Systems Analysis|TSA]]. These days, people hardly know what MTO is, because we haven't done that much over the last 20 years. We're not going to be able to micromanage every single sortie."


Air Tasking Order-style tasking
>“Two F-15Es strike Target A at 0315Z with these weapons, this tanker plan, this route, and this support package.”

Mission-Type Order-style tasking
> “Degrade enemy long-range fires in Area X by 30% before tomorrow’s ground attack. Prioritize mobile launchers and fire-control nodes. Avoid collateral damage in these zones. Coordinate with ISR and fires cells.”
