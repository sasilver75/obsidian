The process of selecting objects or installations to be attacked, taken, or destroyed in warfare.
- Systematically analyzes and prioritizes targets and matches appropriate lethal and nonlethal actions to those targets to create specific desired effects that achieve the [[Joint Force Commander]]'s objectives, accounting for operational requirements, capabilities, and the results of previous assessments.
- The emphasis is on identifying [[High Value Target]]s, resources that the enemy can least afford to use, or that provide him with the greatest advantage, and then further selecting the subset of those targets that must be acquired/engaged to achieve *friendly success* ([[High Payoff Target|High-Payoff Target]]s). Targeting thus links desired effects to actions and tasks.

> Targeting: The process of selecting and prioritizing targets, and matching the appropriate response to them, considering operational requirements and capabilities.

> Target: An entity or object that performs a function for the adversary or enemy and is considered for possible engagement.

> Object: A digital representation of a real-world physical thing, action, or abstraction, that serves as as single point of convergence for intelligence production to facilitate analysis. 
> Object-based production (OBP): The intelligence community's framework for organizing and sharing information, relating data from all sources to known objects. 

Entity Types: Facilities, Individuals, Virtual, Equipment, Organizations
Target Characteristics: Physical, Functional, Cognitive Control and Information, Environmental, Temporal


The targeting process can be grouped into two categories:
- [[Deliberate Targeting]]: Prosecutes anticipated or known targets within a given operational area and timeframe, and normally supports the joint force's [[Future Plans]] effort, which is overseen by the plans directorate of a joint staff (J-5).
	- Normally the [[Future Operations]] directorate focuses on 24 hours up to 72 hours.
	- ==Scheduled Targets==: Planned targets upon which fires or other actions are scheduled for prosecutation at a specified time; may appear on an [[Air Tasking Order]] to synchronize actions with a specific delivery or time on target.
	- ==On-Call Targets==: Planned targets upon which fires are determined using the deliberate targeting process and executed using the dynamic targeting process.  These targets may have dedicated capabilities listed against them on the [[Aviation Technology Office|ATO]], but no specific deliveryr time or time on target is identified. The command expects to find, fix, and track targets during the execution period to prosecute them.
- [[Dynamic Targeting]]: Prosecutes targets which were not included in the deliberate targeting process, possibly because they were not known or not initially selected for prosecution. Dynamic targeting is normally employed in [[Current Operations]] because the nature and time frame associated with current operations (usually the current 24-hour period) typically requires more immediate responsiveness compared to deliberate targeting.
	- ==Unscheduled Target==: Targets of opportunity known to exist in the operating environment. They *may* have had the same amount of target development as planned targets and reside on the joint target list ([[Joint Target List|JTL]]) or the restricted target list ([[Restricted Target List|RTL]]); however, they were neither nominated nor approved for inclusion on the joint integrated prioritized target list ([[Joint Integrated Prioritized Target List|JIPTL]]), or were not available for engagement within the execution period. 
	- ==Unanticipated Target==: Targets of opportunity that were unknown or unexpected in the operating environment. These targets may not have been developed in the deliberate targeting process nor included on a JTL or RTL;; Nevertheless, an evaluation of the candidate target is required before it can be engaged.

Special Considerations:
- [[Sensitive Target]]: Targets for which planned actions warrant Presidential or SecWar approval. 
- [[High Value Target|High-Value Target]]: A target that the *enemy* requires for the successful completion of *their* mission.
- [[High Payoff Target|High-Payoff Target]]: Typically a subset of HVTs, a target whose loss to the enemy could significantly contribute to the success of the friendly course of action. Given their importance, the JFC and component prioritize these targets.
- [[Time Sensitive Target|Time-Sensitive Target]]: A [[Joint Force Commander|JFC]]-validated target or set of targets requiring immediate response, because it's a highly lucrative, fleeting target of opportunity, or poses a danger to friendly forces.
- [[Component-Critical Target]]: HPTs taht pose a threat to their mission or force for cross-component execution. Typically considered a type of HPT and are nominated by Component commanders as TSTs.


The [[Joint Targeting Cycle]] is a six-phase iterative process:
1. End state and commander's objectives
2. Target development and prioritization
3. Capabilities analysis
4. Commander's decision and force assignment
5. Mission planning and force execution
6. Assessment

==Target Acquisition== is the detection and identification of the location of a target in sufficient detail to permit the effective employment of lethal/non-lethal means. 

> The Joint Targeting Process categorizes adversary threat elements into Target Systems, Target System Components, and Target Elements


In [[Contested, Degraded, and Operationally Limited]] environments, units may have to continue operations and targeting responsibilities despite the lack of continuous communications with higher headquarters (HHQ). 

# Air Component

Five fundamental principles guide the air component's targeting process:
- ==Objectives-based targeting==: The entire process focuses on achieving the [[Joint Force Commander|JFC]]'s objectives, and that all targets nominated for engagement should contribute in a discernible way to attaining the JFC's desired end state.
- ==Effects-based targeting==: Effects are created to achieve objectives.
- ==Interdisciplinary==: Targeting requires expertise of personnel from many functional disciplines, from strategists, operators, and intelligence analysts.
- ==Systemic==: The air component executes a rational, iterative, and methodical targeting process.
- ==Estimative==: Predicting future outcomes to encourage planners to understand an enemy's decision-making cycle and anticipate the effects of operations.
