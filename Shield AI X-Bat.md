> "Create endless dilemmas for adversaries; capable of launching and recovering from ships, remote islands, or austere forward bases while eliminating dependency on traditional infrastructure."

> "Vertical takeoff and landing meets 2,000+ NM of long range multi-mission dominance.Can carry both air-to-air and air-to-surface weapons in its internal bays. It can also carry large strike weapons on its external hardpoints."

> "3:1 Expeditionary Footprint": Three X-Bats fit in the deck space of one legacy fighter, dramatically increasing sortie generation and operational tempo."

==Not yet in service. First flight in 2026, mission capability by 2028, and production in 2029.==

> "V-Bat's a harder thing to land than X-Bat, actually; you're operating in the hardest weather conditions with a small amount of thrust; X-Bat has lots of multi-plane thrust vectoring, which lets us put it where we need to put it."

> Re: Manufacturability: "2029, we're targeting 50 aircraft, but ramping to 300-500 aircraft over a number of years. The demand for the aircraft will play a role in that. Customer reception has been wild, though. It's a non-obvious way to solve a lto of problems that weren't articulated as problems. 'What does this mean for the Navy and Marine Crops and Air Force when your strike power is no longer limited to a runway... when you can have a strike capability based on a small island chain.' It will be as transformational as trying to launch airplanes from ships, which basically defined the military force structure of the US for the next 80 years."
