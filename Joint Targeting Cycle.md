The US joint force's structured process for turning a commander's objectives into prioritized targets, matched capabilities, execution orders, and assessment feedback.

The Joint Targeting Cycle supports both [[Deliberate Targeting]] and [[Dynamic Targeting]].


Six Phases:
1. ==End State and Commander's Objectives==: Define what the commander is trying to achieve and what effects are needed.
	- "What military or political conditions are we trying to create?" -> "Reduce the adversary's ability to launch theater ballistic missiles against allied bases for the the next 72 hours."
2. ==Target Development and Prioritization==: Identify, validate, restrict, and prioritize targets that could help create those effects.
	- Turn that objective into candidate targets, asking:
		- What adversary systems enable the unwanted behavior?
		- Which nodes in that system matters the most?
		- Which targets are valid military targets?
		- which targets are legally or politically restricted?
		- Which targets are worth acting on first?
	- Outputs:
		- [[Candidate Target List]] (CTL): Proposed targets still being developed or considered.
		- [[Joint Target List]] (JTL): Validated targets with no special engagement restrictions.
		- [[Restricted Target List]] (RTL): Validated targets that may be engaged only under specific restrictions.
		- [[No-Strike List]] (NSL): Entities protected from military effects under law, rules of engagement, or policy. Strictly, this is not a target list.
		- [[Joint Integrated Prioritized Target List]] (JIPTL): The prioritized list of targets approved by the Joint Force Commander for action.
		- [[Target Development Nomination]] (TDN): A proposal to develop or add a target for approval.
3. ==Capabilities Analysis==: Decide what lethal or nonlethal capabilities could create the desired effect on each target.
	- Given the desired effect and target characteristics, what capability should we use? Broader than just [[Weaponeering]], because it can include cyber effects, electronic warfare, space effects, special operations, deception or isolation, etc.
	- This phase also considers collateral damage, civilian harm mitigation, rules of engagement, weapon availability, probability of success, fratricide risk, escalation risk, timing.
4. ==Commander's Decision and Force Assignment==: Get approval and assign targets or effects to components, units, or capabilities.
	- The Joint Force Commander (or delegated authority) decides which targets are approved, which effects matter most, which components get assigned tasks, and which scarce assets get allocated.
	- In an air-heavy context, this connects to:
		- [[Joint Targeting Coordination Board]] (JTCB): A forum for synchronizing component targeting priorities with the Joint Force Commander's objectives.
		- [[Joint Fires Element]] (JFE): A staff element that helps integrate joint fires and target prioritization.
		- [[Joint Force Air Component Commander]] (JFACC): The commander often responsible for planning and controlling joint air operations.
		- [[Air Operations Directive]] (AOD): Air component guidance expressing intent, apportionment, desired effects, target priorities, ISR needs, and assessment measures.
		- [[Master Air Attack Plan]] (MAAP): The time-phased air component scheme of maneuver for an [[Air Tasking Order]] period.
5. ==Mission Planning and Force Execution==: Turn approved target decisions into executable missions and carry them out.
	- This phase turns approved targeting decisions into executable missions.
	- For air operations, this often flows into the [[Air Tasking Cycle]]: target nominations, [[Weaponeering]] and allocation, [[Master Air Attack Plan]], [[Air Tasking Order]], [[Airspace Control Order]], special instructions, unit-level mission planning, and finally execution.
6. ==Assessment==: Determine whether the action worked, whether the objective is closer, and whether re-attack or adjustment is needed.
	- Did the action create the desired effect, and what should we do next?
	- Layers to this:
		- [[Battle Damage Assessment]] (BDA)
		- Physical Damage Assessment (PDA)
		- Functional Damage Assessment (FDA)
		- Target System Damage Assessment (TSDA)
		- Munitions Effectiveness Assessment (MEA)
		- Collateral Damage Assessment (CDA)
		- Re-attack Recommendation
