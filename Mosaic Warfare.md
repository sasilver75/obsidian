The [[Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency|DARPA]] [[Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency|Strategic Technology Office]]'s concept for force design and operations to enable 21st century maneuver warfare.
- (Note that this is a different concept than the IRGC's "Mosaic Defense", which is about decentralized leadership)

A "mosaic" is a system where pieces are designed to be flexibly networked and rapidly configured to provide resilient capabilities to the operator. Like the tiles in a mosaic, any system (or unit) having certain functional characteristics could be combined with others to provide a desired warfighting capability at the time and place of a commander's choosing.

A larger diversity of attack options across domains provides entirely new approaches to preempt, dislocate, or disrupt an adversary. A skilled mosaic commander at the tactical and operational level will employ an ever-changing disposition coupled with the ability to rapidly control and scale signature, combat power, and logistical support to create dilemmas for the adversary, exploit positional advantage, and control tempo.

Mosaic can be achieved through a composite of existing systems (e.g. better quadcopter and fires integration at lower echelons) but requires advances in command and control to manage increasingly complex [[Kill Web]]s. Unlike [[Kill Chain]]s, webs dynamically source effects from across a network of options based on shifting mission demands. The more intelligence, fires, and logistics assets available to small formations, the more units will need some form of AI-enabled command control and mission planning applications to manage the kill web. This is especially true when small units will be able to fire munitions that can target ground, air, and sea targets.

Military planners refer to a decision cycle known as the [[OODA Loop]]; it's well known that a military that can get inside the OODA loop of its opponent will win. Continuous speed is an imperative.

Continuous speed applies in all time domains, not just ==battle time==. IN operational ==planning time==, and even acquisition ==development time== domains, where we're also challenge. 

Our system of systems today: All edges are carefully engineered to fit together in just one way; if you lose one piece, you lose the entire effect, and putting things together is difficult and complex.

Real [[Multi-Domain Operations|Multi-Domain]] battle... needs our [[Command and Control|C2]] infrastructure to be built around mission sets and time. Time is the factor... for the whole [[Find, Fix, Track, Target, Engage, Assess|F2T2EA]] chain. Different capabilities from different domains operate on different time schedules (e.g. space-to-surface ISR at one pace, HUMINT at another pace, Cyber at a different pace (not including what it takes to be prepared to be on the network when you need to be), airplanes give it to you at another pace)... so maybe the future of our command and control should rest in mission sets and time, rather than domains and geography...

Because we have to get space-to-surface ISR and F-22 sensor suites to give data to a SOF team that's covert and behind enemy lines in real time. Why do we not have radars that collate to create a common operating picture ([[ANTPY-2|AN/TPY-2]], [[Aegis Combat System|Aegis]], [[F-22 Raptor|F-22]], [[B-21 Raider|B-21]] radars, etc...)... We've got to get to the point where it's seamless. And right now... they largely *do not talk.* An on-orbit capability can't talk to an F-22, can't talk to an AEGIS, can't talk to the TOC. To start with... we need to look at technologies for gateways and translators to be able to present integrated data in a visualization to commanders... How to offboard all the data from an F-22, B-21, etc.
