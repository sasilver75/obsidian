A(n often) hierarchical structure that allows us to classify things.

For example, the Linean Taxonomy is the hierarchical system for classifying and naming organisms, e.g. (Domain, Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species). You can then classify individuals by describing which of these categories that individual belongs to.

An [[Ontology]] is a set of n-tuples or a web of concepts that helps computers or AI "understand" your data. Gives you the conceptual web, the conceptual structure, that maps up with the way that humans think about these concepts. An Ontology includes taxonomical concepts, often, but also includes the relationships that things can hold to eachother. I don't just want to know what type of animal it is, but also the various anatomical body parts it has, and how they're attached to eachother, etc. So the Taxonomy gives us the skeleton, and an Ontology fleshes it out. Often times, Ontologies *are graphs.* Often embodied as something like a [[Knowledge Graph]]. Semantics is the buzzword that all of these things fit under... where we care about the meaning, rather than the structure. When you develop a new Logic, you have to say what symbols there are in the logical language (the syntax), and then we also need an interpretation; we can build a syntactically-correct sentence, but what does it mean? This is the semantics.
