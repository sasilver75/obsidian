The [[United States Army|Army]]'s structured, seven-step analytical planning methodology used by commanders and staff to understand a situation, develop potential [[Course of Action]]s (COAs), and produce executional [[Operational Plan]]s (OPLANs) and [[Operational Order]]s (OPORDs).


1. Receipt of Mission
	- The commander and staff receive or anticipate a mission, start the planning timeline, issue initial guidance, and begin preparing the staff to plan.
	- "What did we receive, how much time do we have, and how do we start planning without wasting time?"
	- Staff may only have a planning timeline, at this point.
2. Mission analysis
	- Staff studies the higher mission, enemy, terrain, friendly forces, constraints, risks, and assumptions to understand the problem and define what must be accomplished. Outputs might include the restated mission, commander's intent, commander's critical information requirements, initial risk assessment, mission analysis briefing, updated running estimates, planning guidance, and a [[Warning Order|WARNORD]] to subordinates.
	- "What is the actual problem we must solve?" There may be a specified task, implied task, essential task, constraint, restraint, assumptions.
	- These become inputs to the SYNCMATs.
3. Course of Action (COA) Development
	- Staff creates distinct, *suitable, feasible, acceptable, suitable, distinguishable, and complete* ways to accomplish the mission. Output might include COA statement, COA sketch, task organization, initial synchronization matrix, scheme of maneuver, initial fires, intelligence, sustainment, and protection concepts.
	- "What are the plausible ways we can accomplish this mission?"
	- Staff builds an initial synchronization matrix for each proposed COA.
4. COA Analysis (War-Gaming)
	- Staff tests each course of action against likely enemy actions and battlefield friction to identify risks, decision points, resource conflicts, a and required refinements. Output might be refined COAs, decision points, branches and sequels, synchronization matrix, decision-support template/matrix, high-payoff target list, updated risk assessment, identified information requirements.
	- "What happens when this plan collides with the enemy, terrain, time uncertainty, and friction?"
	- Staff uses and refines the SYNCMAT while testing friendly action, enemy reaction, friendly counteraction
5. COA Comparison
	- The staff compares the analyzed COAs against the evaluation criteria so that the commander can see the tradeoffs clearly. Might output a decision matrix with advantages/disadvantages of each COA, and a staff recommendation.
	- "Which  COA is best, and what tradeoffs does each COA impose?"
	- Staff can compare COAs partly by looking at timing, risk, resource conflicts, and feasibility shown in the SYNCMAT.
6. COA Approval
	- The commander selects, modifies, or rejects a COA and gives final planning guidance for turning a selected COA into an order. Results in an approved COA, refined commander's intent, commander's final planning guidance, updated commander's critical information requirements, [[Warning Order|WARNORD]] to subordinate units.
	- "Which plans are we committing to, and what changes must be made before publishing the order?"
	- Commander selects or modifies a COA; the selected COA's SYNCMAT becomes a basis for detailed planning.
7. Orders Production
	- The staff writes and distributes the [[Operational Order|OPORD]] or [[Fragmentary Order|FRAGO]], then briefs or hands off the plan to the units that must execute it. Might include annexes, appendices, overlays and graphics, the synchronization matrix, execution checklist, confirmation briefs, rehearsal information.
	- "How do we communicate the plan clearly enough so that subordinate units can execute it?"
	- The refined SYNCMAT helps generate the actual operation order, annexes, coordinating instructions, fires plans, sustainment plans, and decision-support products.



Q: It seems like there are multiple [[Warning Order|WARNORD]]s that can be generated, is that interesting?
A: Yeah, they can be typically emitted after receipt of mission, after mission analysis, and after COA approval. Subordinate units themselves need time to plan and prepare; if higher headquarters waited until the final OPORD, subordinate units would lose most of *their* planning time. These WARNORDs let subordinates start acting on partial but useful information.
