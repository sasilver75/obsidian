Pron. "Sib-Urr", rhyming with "shiver"

They connect small, for-profit US businesses with military branches to develop dual-use technologies addressing critical national security needs.

STTRs are like SBIRs but require partnership with colleges, universities, or other federally-funded R&D centers (FFRDCs)


There aer multiple phases to SBIR programs, which follow a structured three-phase process to transition early stage lab concepts into commercial products:
1. Phase I: Feasibility and Proof of Concept (SIBR PHI)
	- Determines the scientific/technical/commercial merit of an idea; $50-300k over 6-12 months.
2. Phase II: Research and Development (SIBR PHII)
	- Expands on Phase I results to deliver working prototype; 750k-2m over 24 months.
3. Phase III: Commercialization (SIBR PHIII)
	- Transitions developed technology into the commercial market or military use; No SBIR funds are used, funding is done by private investors, commmercial sales, or non-SBIR federal contracts. Duration is open-ended.
