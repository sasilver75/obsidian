PrSM pronounced "Prism"

A series of [[Short-Range Ballistic Missile]]s from [[Lockheed Martin]] to replace the [[MGM-140 Army Tactical Missile System]] (ATACMS)
- Increment One is currently in service with the [[United States Army|Army]]
- Increment Two will have anti-ship targeting capability.
- Later increment variants will have modular payloads capabilities and increased range out to 620 mi.

The PRsM is currently only capable of being fired from a [[M142 High Mobility Artillery Rocket System|M142 HIMARS]] launcher.
- Plans are being explored to make it capable of being fired from surface ships via the [[Mk 41 VLS]].

The missile was first used in combat by the United States in 2026 during [[Operation Epic Fury]].
- Unfortunately, this was the weapon used in a missile strike on an Iranian elementary school.

Price: $1.6 million/unit for Increment One
### Increments
- Increment One
	- The current missile in use by the Army. Treaty-bound range of 310mi.
		- Early framework for this program was in 2016, what was 3 years before the US withdrew from the INF treaty, which banned development of ballistic/cruise missiles with ranges >= 500km.
- Increment Two
	- Features a multi-mode seeker consisting of radio frequency and imaging infrared to engage desired target sets in an [[Anti-Access Area Denial|A2AD]] environment.
	- A land-based anti-missile ship able to engage maritime moving targets, as well as relocatable land targets.
	- [[Initial Operating Capability|IOC]] scheduled for FY28
- Increment Three
	- Will include most of the same technology as increments one and two. Its main addition is the extension of the variety of armaments the missile can carry, with it likely carrying more explosive munitions.
	- Designed to be used as an anti-fortification weapon, likely being able to destroy hardened structures.
- Increment Four
	- A focus on the extension of the range of the missile, likely extending the range to 620mi or more.
- Increment Five
	- To design a missile that can be fired from an autonomous vehicle.
	- The idea is to build something longer than an [[M270 Multiple Launch Rocket System|MLRS]] pod...so you might be able to achieve ranges that, right now, don't seem realistic.

Army plans to purchase 3,986 Inc 1 missiles, followed by 1,589 Inc 2 missiles as soon as their production begins.
- Production on Inc 1 is still spinning up as we speak, 250 will be delivered in 2026... though this figure was cut to 152 as a result of a funding decrease, which might indicate some Army internal debate over funding (e.g. [[Long-Range Hypersonic Weapon|Dark Eagle]], a longer-range hypersonic conventional weapon).
