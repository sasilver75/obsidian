Developed by [[General Atomics]] as a replacement fo the [[MQ-1B Predator]], though now the Gray Eagle itself is being obsolesced.
The Army is currently actively shopping for a [[Group 4 UAS]] or [[Group 5 UAS]] replacement with 

Was introduced in the middle of of a counterinsurgency mission, and typically operated in uncontested airspace.
- They are susceptible to medium or high-altitude air defense; MANPADS are less of a threat at a service ceiling of 20-25,000 feet.
- It's possible to use Synthetic Aperture Radar to spot military targets from a long distance;
- Tests of using the [[GBU-69B|GBU-69/B Small Glide Munition]]  has been used to extend the Gray Eagle's strike range from 8 to 32km.
	- Small air-launched drones called "Air-launched effects" could act as jammers, decoys, or weapons themselves to extend the usage of Gray Eagles, allowing them to stay out of the range of some ground-based air-defense systems.

The Gray Eagle requires a 4500x100ft runway, which is about 1.4km long. 
This compares to older versions of the [[RQ-7 Shadow]], which could launch from a catapult and land on a 710x50ft runway.


![[Pasted image 20260716184301.png]]
Gray Eagles are held in 15 UAS companies; 11 are part of combat aviation brigades.
- Two are part of [[Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response|SOAR]]
- Two are part of the 116th Military Intelligence Brigade ("Aerial Exploitation Brigade")
- There are also 11 training aircraft and some additional "float" aircraft.

![[Pasted image 20260716184354.png]]
When deployed, each company operates one Gray Eagle system including 12x MQ-1C aircraft, 6x Universal Ground Control Stations (UGCS), distributed amongst 3 flight platoons, capable of operating independently from different airstrips.
- Some SOF groups then have a 4th flight platoon, but no additional UAVs, allowing for more launch site.s
