I've heard it referred to as "Excal", "X-CAL"
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M982_Excalibur

An extended-range guided artillery shell developed in a collaborative effort between the US Army research Laboratory (ARL) and the United States Army Armament Research, Development, and Engineering Center (ARDEC). Developed and manufactured by [[Raytheon]], [[BAE Systems]], and other subs/primes.

It is a [[GPS INS|GPS/INS]] munition capable of being used in close support situations within 75-150 meters of friendly troops.

By 2016, unit costs were reduced to $68,000/round; by October 2018, over 1,400 rounds had been fired in combat.
- This is compared to an $800 unguided [[M777]] shell... but less expensive than a ~$150,000 rocket used in a [[M142 High Mobility Artillery Rocket System|HIMARS]] or [[M270 Multiple Launch Rocket System|MLRS]].
- A lower cost alternative is to use the [[M1156 Precision Guidance Kit]] (PGK) to turn existing 155mm shells into precision weapons!

> What's interesting is how much range Excalibur adds by itself, simply by flying it slightly nose-up to generate a lifting-body effect; you basically trade velocity for range. The wings also add an appreciable amount of lift on their own.
