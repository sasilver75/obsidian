A 1993 dinner at the [[Pentagon]] attended by representatives of major US defense contractors to discuss industry consolidation in the wake of plans to decrease defense spending after the end of the Cold War, when people were calling for a peace dividend.

Representatives from defense contractors were told that the US government would be unable to support the existing number of firms in the industry, and that projections indicated only a few in each sector would survive upcoming budget costs.

By 1997, the "Big Five" ([[Lockheed Martin]], [[Boeing]], [[Raytheon]], [[Northrop Grumman]], [[General Dynamics]]) consolidations were complete. The number of major contractors had declined from 51 to 5, more than 90%.
