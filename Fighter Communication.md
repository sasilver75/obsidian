In fighter communications, brevity is important, because it lets many parties share a single radio link.

On an [[Air Tasking Order]], every formation gets a callsign for the day, which is the keystone for brevity. Every radio transmission begins with a callsign of the entity being spoken to, followed by the speaking entity's callsign, then the message content will follow.

![[Pasted image 20260725233204.png]]

"Overlord, Eagle 21, Fuel Red."

Some exceptions:
- If an entity is directed to do something: 
- ![[Pasted image 20260725233232.png]]
- If Hornet 1 wanted to tell Hornet 2 to do something, they might just say "Hornet 2, target North Group."

A transmission can also be relayed by a third party:
- The relaying entity omits their callsign and just uses the ones involved in the transmission:

- Here, HOG1 is trying to contact 1, but the message is relayed by Snake 11, who doesn't include their own call sign in the relay.
