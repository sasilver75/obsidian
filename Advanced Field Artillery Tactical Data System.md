The [[United States Army|Army]] (and [[United States Marine Corps|Marine Corps]]) software for fires, providing reliability in coordinating, controlling, and executing fires and effects.

Created in the early 1980s, becoming an official system of record in 1996, when it was first fielded by the [[United States Army|Army]] and [[United States Marine Corps|Marine Corps]].
- Since its creation, it's underwent at least 13 separate upgrades.
- By 2010, the software behind it has "14 or 16 different coding languages," a "pile of spaghetti."
- In [2025](https://www.army.mil/article/283097/afatds_gets_an_upgrade) ... new software upgrade, dubbed AFATDS Artillery Execution Suite (AXS)... which made it much more user-friendly and fast.


# Comparison with [[Integrated Battle Command System|IBCS]]
- IBCS is the modernized network designed to integrate *defensive* air and missile defense sensors and weapons into a single unified picture.
- AFATDS is used to plan, coordinate, and execute offensive artillery and missile strikes, coordinating howitzers,[[M142 High Mobility Artillery Rocket System|HIMARS]]s, and attack aviation.
