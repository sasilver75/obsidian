A [[Unmanned Aerial System|UAV]] capable of [[Short Takeoff and Landing]] (STOL), being developed by [[General Atomics]]

First unveiled in December 2021, its role is to fulfill the role of reconnaissance, CAS, and armed overwatch, but to be able to operate from short, austere runways (say, in the Pacific). It can also be outfitted with various electronic systems (SIGINT-related, moving target indication, [[Synthetic Aperture Radar|SAR]], [[Optical|EO]] sensors).
- It can take off from runways as short as 152 meters in length and carry 12 [[AGM-114 Hellfire]] missiles with 9 hours of endurance.
	- The longest it can fly is 27 hours when using a long runway and no payload, or it can carry a full 3,600 lb payload totaling 16 Hellfire missiles.

The two main things that set it apart from the rest of the [[MQ-1B Predator]] family are its wings and landing gear.
- The wings are enlarged, featuring "high-lift devices," including leading edge slats, double-slotted flaps, and drooping ailerons.
- The landing gear is also more reinforced when compared to other UAVs, featuring wide bushwheel-style tires, used to assist on landign in rough terrain, and thick shock absorbers as well as more pronounced torque links.

It's also transportable; it can be broken down and transported by a [[C-130 Hercules]], then be assembled and ready for a mission is around 1.5 hours with a four-person team.

Mojave is being used to demonstrate STOL capabilities for the upcoming MQ-9B STOL, a wing-kit that allows the MQ-9B (which has greater payload and endurance than the Mojave) to operate from austere runways and aircraft carriers.
