Terms:
- "Hot Pit" or "Hot Gas": Refueling aircraft while the engine is running; the pilot doesn't get out of the plane.
- An "Integrated Combat Turn" is a Hot Pit while also the aircraft. You might pull over the [[MJ-1]] Jammer to help reload bombs, or for certain planes like the A-10, if you want to reload the GAU-8, you need the Dragon loader.  Can often be knocked out in ~20 minutes.
- A "FARP" is a Forward Area Refueling Point or Forward Arming and Refueling Point. For the Air Force, we're more focused on the first one; we're going to have a [[C-130 Hercules]] to set down the FARP guys (Petroleum, Oil, and Lubricants guys ([[Air Force Speciality Code|AFSC]]: 2F0X1) to roll a hose off and start refueling A-10s from an internal gas tank on that C-13. They can also put a fuel bladder in the cargo bed and refuel from there.
	- If there's a fuel bladder in that C-130, you don't have room for the bombs, so if you want to bring the bombs, you need to have a separate C-130...
	- Your engine is still running, like in a Hot Pit.
	- Marine Corps is doing a lot of this type of thinking/work for their CV-22s, Sea Stallions, etc.
- "Contingency Location" (CL): Wherever the hell you are when you're doing these missions (Hot Pit, ICTs, FARPs). So when "We're going to do CL operations," they really mean "We're going to *open* a CL, and then do one of the Hot Pit/ICT/FARP missions above". This could be a friendly base, a former bad guy airport that we've taken over, or a stretch of highway. Only designed to be set up for 2-3 days. If you've got 20 dudes with you, and you set up a fuel bladder... host nation fuel trucks, etc... If I'm out there, the most important people out there are your crew chiefs and POL guys. If you had 20 security forces guys... that's cool, but now we're just at the side of a highway not doing anything.
