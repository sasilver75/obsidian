Something used in a [[Guided Multiple Launch Rocket System|GMLRS]] or [[M142 High Mobility Artillery Rocket System|HIMARS]] launcher, underwing off an airplane, or vertically from a ship.
Mixture cast into a solid mold, but into a casing, lit on fire, and it propels itself based on that.

All rockets used some form of solid or powdered propellant ("Solid-Propellant Rocket") until the 20th century, when [[Liquid-Propellant Rocket]] offered more efficient and controllable alternatives. Still, because of their simplicity (and storability), solid rockets are still used today in military armaments worldwide.
- Solid-fuel rockets can remain in storage for an extended period (>10 years) without much propellant degradation, since they almost always launch reliably. The lower *performance* of solid-propellant rockets means that they aren't typically used as the primary propulsion for medium-to-large launch vehicles. Still, solid rockets can be used for light launch vehicles for [[Low Earth Orbit|LEO]] payloads under 2 tons, or escape payloads up to 500kg.
- Once ignited, a simple solid rocket motor cannot be shut off, as it contains all the ingredients necessary for combustion within the chamber in which they are burned.

==Pulsed Rocket Motors== burn in segments, and can be ignited upon command.

Ammonium perchlorate is used to supply oxygen; Aluminum is usually used at fuel because it burns at high temperature and is inexpensive, so it's widely used in solid rockets. In powder form for each, they are mixed, along with a binder liquid. A hollow space must be made in the center of the solid propellant, so the liquid propellant is poured into a mold and hardened; The shape of the hollow space can be used in various ways, for different performance characteristics.
