Something used in a [[Guided Multiple Launch Rocket System|GMLRS]] or [[M142 High Mobility Artillery Rocket System|HIMARS]] launcher, underwing off an airplane, or vertically from a ship.
Mixture cast into a solid mold, but into a casing, lit on fire, and it propels itself based on that.

All rockets used some form of solid or powdered propellant ("Solid-Propellant Rocket") until the 20th century, when [[Liquid-Propellant Rocket]] offered more efficient and controllable alternatives. Still, because of their simplicity (and storability), solid rockets are still used today in military armaments worldwide.
- Solid-fuel rockets can remain in storage for an extended period without much propellant degradation, since they almost always launch reliably. The lower *performance* of solid-propellant rockets means that they aren't typically used as the primary propulsion for medium-to-large launch vehicles. Still, solid rockets can be used for light launch vehicles for [[Low Earth Orbit|LEO]] payloads under 2 tons, or escape payloads up to 500kg.
- Once ignited, a simple solid rocket motor cannot be shut off, as it contains all the ingredients necessary for combustion within the chamber in which they are burned.

==Pulsed Rocket Motors== burn in segments, and can be ignited upon command.
