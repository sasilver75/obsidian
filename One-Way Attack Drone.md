A self-destructive [[Unmanned Autonomous System|UAS]] designed to strike a target by crashing into it with an integrated warhead. 

One of the most defining characteristics is their low cost relative to traditional [[Cruise Missile]]s; a [[Kalibr]] cruise missile might cost upwards of $1 million, while a [[Shahed 136]] might cost between $20,000 and $50,000. This cost asymmetry lets operators launch them in "Swarms" to deplete or penetrate enemy air defenses, whose air-defense missiles like [[MIM-104 Patriot]]s cost significantly more than the drone they're destroying. as a result, a combination of low-cost interceptor drones, electronic jamming, anti-aircraft cannons, and low-cost missiles such as [[Advanced Precision Kill Weapon System|APKWS]] are used against such drone targets.



Comparison with [[Loitering Munition]]:
- "Loitering munitions typically feature human-in-the-loop control and the ability to loiter or "hunt" for targets before striking remotely by a human operator, while one-way attack drones are often launched against pre-programmed coordinates."
	- ((I think in reality there's some blurring.))
- Loitering munitions like the [[Lancet]] are designed to loiter over a target area for an extended period, using onboard sensors to locate targets of opportunity. They typically require a human-in-the-loop to select the target and approve the strike at the final stage.
- One-Way Attack Drones like the [[Shahed 136]] or [[Low-Cost Uncrewed Combat Attack System|LUCAS]] typically utilize [[Global Navigation Satellite System|GNSS]] and [[Inertial Navigation System]]s to fly to specific, pre-programmed geographical coordinates, and generally do not have the ability the loiter, hunt for moving targets, or return to base if a target is not found.
	- These are additionally distinct because they are often employed for strategic [[Fires]] against static infrastructure deep behind enemy lines, rather than as tactical battlefield support.
